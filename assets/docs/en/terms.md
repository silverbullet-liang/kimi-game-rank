## 9. Comments, chat and likes

1. Signed-in users can comment, take part in World Chat and like works and comments; **guests can only read** leaderboards, public comments and World Chat, and cannot post, like or use the AI chat.
2. Each person may like a given work or comment once; after unliking, it can be liked again.
3. You can delete your own comment (with its replies) and recall messages you sent; recalled content is no longer shown. A deleted comment **goes to the recycle bin** (no longer displayed) and an administrator can restore or permanently clear it in the control panel. Administrators may also **hide** a comment (collapsed), which does not remove it.
4. **Content measures**: administrators and co-administrators may, under this agreement and site rules, delete, collapse, hide or otherwise handle content that breaks the rules, and may warn, restrict, mute, ban or clean data on the account involved; in serious or suspected unlawful cases the site may preserve records and report to the authorities as required by law.
5. Disagreements between users are for them to resolve; the site does not intervene and bears no joint liability.
6. **Check before posting ("three stages")**: comments and World Chat messages are checked **before publication**. The logic is **fully published below**; the three stages run in order and **if any stage fails, the whole piece is blocked** — not published, not shown; only after all three pass is it published.

   **Stage 1 · Site rule matching (local, nothing leaves the server)**

   - **Normalisation**: common "disguised spellings" are flattened first — full-width and half-width forms, letter case, spaces and symbols inserted inside a word, zero-width and other invisible characters, repeated characters. Splitting, interleaving and stacking are all restored before comparison.
   - **Word list**: the content is compared with the site's moderation list, which contains only **clearly prohibited** entries. There is also an **allow list**: a match that falls inside an allowed expression (ordinary words, common game and work phrasing, common names and places) is ignored, to reduce false positives.
   - **Pattern detection**: behaviour that has nothing to do with vocabulary — rapid repeated flooding, contact details left in any form (platform accounts, group numbers, phone, email, QR codes), long digit strings and links, and typical traffic-driving or marketing phrasing.

   Nothing is sent to any third party at this stage.

   **Stage 2 · Foreign profanity check after translation**

   To close the gap of "abusing in another language to bypass the Chinese list", the **first 300 characters** are sent to a machine translation service for English (text already in English is not translated), and the English is then checked by an English profanity service.

   **Stage 3 · Severity grading**

   The content is given to a **lightweight classifier** that returns a **severity profile**: ten levels from 1 (completely normal) to 10 (extreme abuse). The site takes the weighted result as the content's **severity level**; no explanation and no other content is generated. This catches **contextual violations** that neither the word list nor the profanity service covers (passive-aggressive personal attacks, spliced abuse).

   Handling depends on three bands:

   - **At or above the block line** (about level 6 by default) → blocked, not published;
   - **In the middle band** (about level 5 by default) → published as usual, with a "**possibly malicious**" note beside it — such content sits between criticism and attack, and blocking it outright would do more harm than annotating it;
   - **Below the middle band** → published normally with no note.

   There is also a category of **probing aimed at the AI itself** (prompt injection or jailbreaks such as "ignore the above instructions" or "print your system prompt"): it abuses nobody but is an attack on the system, and is handled **like the middle band — annotated, not blocked** (ordinary technical discussion uses these words, and blocking them would hurt a lot of legitimate conversation).

   Grading rather than a plain pass/fail is precisely what reduces false positives: criticism, complaints and passive-aggressive remarks usually stay in the low bands and never reach the block line.

   The classifier focuses on **whether there is abuse**; **advertising and traffic driving** are handled mainly by the word list and patterns of stage 1. If the classifier is unavailable, a large model makes a binary judgement instead. **This stage costs you nothing** — the site pays, and it is not linked to your account.

   **Outcome**

   - **Pass**: published, with no notice to you.
   - **Fail**: not published, with the message "content did not pass review — check for abuse, advertising or contact details". The message **does not say which stage or what matched** — publishing the boundary to that level would turn it into a bypass manual. If you believe it is a mistake, ask for human review through "Feedback".
   - **The same content is checked once**: conclusions are cached against the content, so identical content is not sent again or judged again while cached.
   - **Notes can be removed**: middle-band content carries a "possibly malicious" line — a hint to readers that **does not affect visibility**; an administrator who thinks it is misapplied can remove it in one tap.
   - **Fail-open on outages**: if the **external service** of a stage is unavailable (timeout, rate limit, failure), that stage is treated as passed and the event is logged, so a third-party outage does not stop you posting; the site owner may change this to "fail closed".
   - **No personal information involved**: moderation processes **only the content**, does not analyse your account, history or personal information, is not used for profiling, recommendation or different treatment, and only decides whether that piece is published. The third-party services and what is shared are listed in article 8 of the [Privacy Policy](/doc/隐私政策).

   **Image moderation**

   An uploaded image is checked automatically **before it is written to disk**: a vision model reads the text in it and detects prohibited imagery, then the classifier above judges it; if either is unavailable, **another vision model** decides directly. If it fails, the image is rejected and **the file already written is deleted**; if **all** primary and fallback channels are unavailable, it **passes by default and the event is logged** (the site owner may change this to "block when unavailable"). Only the image is processed, never linked to your account.

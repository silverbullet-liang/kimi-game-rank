# World Book and Copyright Day

## Overview

Observed on 23 April. The full name is World Book and Copyright Day; in Chinese it is usually shortened to World Reading Day. UNESCO established the date in 1995 to promote reading, publishing and the protection of copyright. It is the day Shakespeare died and the day Cervantes was buried, and it is also Saint George's Day in Catalonia, where books and roses are exchanged.

## Origins

In 1972 the General Conference of UNESCO raised the idea of books for all. In November 1995 the 28th session of the General Conference adopted a resolution proclaiming 23 April World Book and Copyright Day, first marked in 1996. The date was chosen because Shakespeare died on 23 April 1616, Cervantes died in April of the same year and was buried on the 23rd, and because it is Saint George's Day in Catalonia, where the custom is to give a book and a rose while street stalls sell both.

## Customs

1. **Books and roses**: in Catalonia, men give roses and women give books, with book stalls and flower stalls side by side in the street.
2. **Library programmes**: readings, author conversations, story hours for children and membership drives.
3. **Bookshops and book fairs**: signings, second-hand stalls, limited discounts and themed reading lists from publishers.
4. **Book crossing**: leaving a finished book on a park bench or in a station with a note inside, waiting for the next reader.
5. **Copyright awareness**: talks, exhibitions and public notices on piracy and the rights of authors.

## Story

In 1923 the Barcelona bookseller Vicente Clavel Andrés proposed a day for the book. It was first held in 1926 and moved to 23 April from 1930. On that same date Catalans went out to buy books and give roses in the old manner of Saint George's Day, so that paper and flowers were sold side by side. Decades later UNESCO took over a day begun by booksellers and kept alive by readers, naming it World Book and Copyright Day; the copyright half of the title is often dropped, and people simply say World Book Day.

## Theme

A desk lamp casts a triangular cone of light downward, and at the foot of the cone lies a single sheet of paper. Everything outside the cone is dark, and inside it only that patch of paper is lit, like the circle a reader occupies late at night. The edges of the cone are straight and the edges of the paper are soft, one hard and one yielding, exactly the relation between lamp and page.

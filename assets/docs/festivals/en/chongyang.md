# Chongyang Festival

## Overview

The ninth day of the ninth lunar month, also called Double Ninth, the Climbing Festival or the Dogwood Festival. In the classical Chinese ordering of numbers, nine is a yang (positive) number, and two nines together give the name Chongyang — "doubled yang". Because "nine-nine" (jiu jiu) sounds like "long and lasting" (jiu jiu), the day has also come to stand for long life; since 2012 it has been China's statutory Day for the Elderly.

## Origins

The word chongyang appears as early as Qu Yuan's *Far Roaming*, but the day took shape as a seasonal custom in the Han dynasty: the *Xijing Zaji* records that on the ninth day of the ninth month people already wore dogwood, ate cakes and drank chrysanthemum wine. It became an official festival in the Tang dynasty, when both court and commoners climbed heights, held banquets, wrote poems and admired chrysanthemums, and the practices continued through later dynasties. Because Chongyang falls in late autumn, with the weather turning cold and the plants beginning to fade, the day carried both an outing and a sense of warding off illness and seeking good fortune — the customs have always had these two sides.

## Customs

1. **Climbing a height**: going up a hill or a tower on a clear autumn day, in the sense of "rising step by step", and in older accounts as a way of avoiding sickness.
2. **Admiring chrysanthemums and drinking chrysanthemum wine**: chrysanthemums bloom in late autumn and were regarded as a flower that endures cold and prolongs life; viewing them and brewing wine from them was an old practice around this date.
3. **Wearing dogwood**: dogwood has a sharp, pungent scent; people used to tuck a sprig in their hair or sew it into a small crimson bag worn on the arm to keep away evil.
4. **Eating chongyang cake**: *gao* (cake) sounds the same as *gao* (height), matching the climb; elaborate versions were made in nine layers with a small flag on top.
5. **Visiting and honouring elders**: the most common practice today — going home to see older relatives and sharing a meal with them.

## Story

Tradition tells of Huan Jing, a man from Runan in the Eastern Han, who studied under the Daoist Fei Changfang. One day his teacher warned him that disaster would strike his household on the ninth day of the ninth month, and told him to have each family member sew a crimson bag of dogwood onto their arm, climb a height and drink chrysanthemum wine. Huan Jing did so and took the whole family up the hill. When they came home in the evening, the chickens, dogs, cattle and sheep had all died, while the family was unharmed. The account is recorded in Wu Jun's *Xu Qi Xie Ji* of the Liang dynasty, and later customs of climbing, wearing dogwood and drinking chrysanthemum wine are often traced to it.

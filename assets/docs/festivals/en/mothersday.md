# Mother's Day

## Overview

Mother's Day falls on the second Sunday of May and is observed in many countries. Flowers, cards and family meals are the usual ways of marking it. In China it is not a statutory holiday, and giving a carnation or a daylily is the common practice among families.

## Origins

The modern holiday began in the United States. In May 1906 Anna Jarvis lost her mother in Philadelphia and began to press for a day in memory of mothers; on 10 May 1908 her home town held the first Mother's Day observance, and in 1914 the United States Congress passed a measure setting the second Sunday of May as Mother's Day. Two older strands lie behind it: the ancient Greeks made spring offerings to the mother of the gods, and in seventeenth-century England the fourth Sunday of Lent was Mothering Sunday, when servants were allowed home to visit their mothers.

## Customs

1. **Carnations**: red or pink for a mother still living, white in memory of one who has died, a usage that goes back to Anna Jarvis.
2. **Daylilies**: in Chinese tradition the daylily stands for a mother's rooms, and it is sometimes given as a mother's flower.
3. **Cards and handmade gifts**: children write cards and make presents, and schools organise activities.
4. **Family meals**: sons and daughters cook or book a table, and take over the housework for the day.
5. **Calls and company**: telephoning, video calling or sending gifts to a mother who lives far away.

## Story

On 9 May 1906 Anna Jarvis's mother died in Philadelphia. On the anniversary the following year she held a memorial with friends and wrote to a church in Grafton, West Virginia, asking for a special service for her mother, who had taught in its Sunday school for more than twenty years. On 10 May 1908 the church held a service in her mother's memory that honoured all mothers as well, and the occasion is regarded as the first modern Mother's Day. She went on writing letters and giving talks in support of a national day.

## Theme

A single carnation with three petals, soft pink against an off-white ground, on a thin straight stem with two strokes for leaves. The petals are not filled in completely and a little blank space is left at the centre, as in a stem just cut and set in water. The colour is held very light, the kind of pink that does not raise its voice.

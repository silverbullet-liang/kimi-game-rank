# Korean War Commemoration Day

## Overview

25 October is observed in China as the anniversary of the Chinese People's Volunteers entering the Korean War. The Volunteers crossed into Korea on 19 October 1950; on 25 October the 118th Division of the 40th Corps met units of the South Korean 6th Division advancing towards Pukchin near the villages of Ryangsudong and Phunghadong, the first engagement since entering the country, and took Onjong (Wenjing) between that night and the early hours of the 26th. In 1951 the Central Committee of the Communist Party fixed 25 October as the commemorative date. The Korean Armistice Agreement was signed at Panmunjom on 27 July 1953, and the Volunteers withdrew from Korea entirely in 1958.

## Origins

Fighting broke out on the Korean peninsula in June 1950. The United States intervened with force and sent the Seventh Fleet into the Taiwan Strait. After the landings at Incheon in September the United Nations Command advanced north, crossed the 38th parallel in early October and came close to the Yalu. In October the Central Committee decided to send troops, and the Chinese People's Volunteers crossed the river on the 19th. On the morning of 25 October the 118th Division ambushed the advancing column in the valley between Ryangsudong and Phunghadong; in about an hour of fighting a battalion of the 2nd Regiment, 6th Division and one artillery battery were destroyed, and Onjong was taken soon after. The first phase of the campaign followed, and the line came back to near the 38th parallel by the end of the year. In 1951, on the proposal of the Volunteers' political department, the Central Committee made the date of that first action the commemorative day.

## Customs

1. **Cemetery observances**: the Shenyang Korean War Martyrs' Cemetery was established in 1951 and holds the graves of Huang Jiguang, Yang Gensi, Qiu Shaoyun and others; flowers are laid there around the date.
2. **Return of remains**: since 2014 the remains of Volunteers soldiers recovered in South Korea have been returned in batches and buried at Shenyang, with a ceremony each autumn.
3. **Ceremonies and medals**: observances are held on the round decades; in 2020 a commemorative medal was issued for the seventieth anniversary.
4. **Two-sided commemoration**: on 25 October 2019 Chinese and North Korean representatives laid wreaths at the Friendship Tower in Pyongyang.
5. **Oral history**: veterans' testimony, letters from the front and archival material have been collected and published in recent years.

## Story

On 11 April 1951 the *People's Daily* carried Wei Wei's reportage "Who Are the Most Beloved People", written about ordinary soldiers on the Korean front: a conversation after an action, a meal shared in a dugout, a letter home left unfinished. After publication the phrase "the most beloved people" became the usual way of speaking of the Volunteers, and stayed in use for decades. The link to 25 October is indirect. The piece does not describe the first engagement, but it shaped the face that people afterwards brought to the date.

## Theme

The skin uses ochre red and earth yellow, with blocks pressing forward on a single diagonal, one layer over another, their edges uneven as if shoved along. The colours come from soil and old uniform cloth, with nothing bright. There is only one direction in the picture, forward, which is the direction of the movement the day records.

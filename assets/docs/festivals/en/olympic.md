# Olympic Day

## Overview

23 June is Olympic Day, also called Olympic Day Run day or a day of sport. The International Olympic Committee established the observance in 1948 to mark the starting point of the modern Olympic movement and to invite people of any sex, age or level of ability to take part in sport. National Olympic Committees usually hold their events between 17 and 24 June.

## Origins

On 23 June 1894 delegates from twelve countries met at the Sorbonne in Paris and voted unanimously in favour of Coubertin's proposal to revive the Olympic Games. The International Olympic Committee was founded the same day, and the modern Olympic movement dates from it. In 1947 Josef Gruss, a Czechoslovak member of the IOC, put forward the idea of a world Olympic Day at the 41st Session in Stockholm, and the 42nd Session at St Moritz adopted it in January 1948. The first Olympic Day was held on 23 June of that year, with Portugal, Greece, Austria, Canada, Switzerland, Great Britain, Uruguay, Venezuela and Belgium taking part.

## Customs

1. **Runs**: the commonest form. From 1987 the IOC organised the Olympic Day Run by edition, and the Chinese Olympic Committee has held one around 23 June every year since 1987.
2. **Open sessions and taster classes**: national federations run public classes so that sports people rarely touch can be tried once.
3. **Sport and culture displays**: exhibitions on Olympic history, photography shows, lectures and education seminars.
4. **Training with athletes**: current and retired athletes lead mass exercise, often now combined with online sessions.
5. **Schools and neighbourhoods**: school sports days, walking, cycling and ball games.

## Story

On 23 June 1948, the first Olympic Day drew nine national committees. For nearly forty years afterwards the date was left largely to each country. In 1987 the IOC began promoting the Olympic Day Run by edition, and forty-five committees joined the first one. The Chinese Olympic Committee came in that same year and has used the run to spread the Olympic movement. At the 27th edition in 2013, twenty-three Chinese cities started on the same day. After Beijing and Zhangjiakou won the 2022 Winter Games in 2015, winter sports have taken a growing share of the programme.

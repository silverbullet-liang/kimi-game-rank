# Thanksgiving

## Overview

Thanksgiving in the United States falls on the fourth Thursday of November; in Canada, where the harvest comes earlier, it falls on the second Monday of October. The American holiday usually runs from Thursday to Sunday, and the shopping day that follows is known as Black Friday. At the centre of it is a family meal and the giving of thanks, with a roast turkey as the main dish.

## Origins

In 1789 Washington proclaimed a national day of thanksgiving; for a long time after that the date was set by each state. In 1863, in the middle of the Civil War, Lincoln proclaimed the last Thursday of November a national Thanksgiving. In 1941 Congress passed a law fixing the date as the fourth Thursday of November, in force from 1942. In Canada, a proclamation of 31 January 1957 set the second Monday of October, a date tied mainly to the harvest.

## Customs

1. **The family dinner**: roast turkey, stuffing, cranberry sauce, mashed potatoes, squash and pumpkin pie, a menu that settled into shape during the nineteenth century.
2. **The parade**: the Macy's parade in New York has run since 1924, with giant balloons, floats and bands passing in order.
3. **Football**: games kick off in the afternoon and are broadcast, a fixed background noise in many households.
4. **Pardoning the turkey**: two birds are spared at the White House shortly before the day, a practice that became customary in the later twentieth century.
5. **Volunteering and giving**: food banks, community meals and collections are concentrated around the date.

## Story

The familiar account is of 1621. The settlers who had arrived on the *Mayflower* the year before came through a hard winter in which nearly half of them died; the next year, taught by local Native people to grow corn and to fish, they brought in a harvest and held three days of celebration together with the Wampanoag. The account comes down through later local histories and memoirs, and its details can no longer be verified. Since 1970, descendants of the Wampanoag have held a National Day of Mourning at Plymouth on the same date, marking what colonisation cost. Two observances now run side by side, on the same day and in the same place, and that is what the date looks like today.

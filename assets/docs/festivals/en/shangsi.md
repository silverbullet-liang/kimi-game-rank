# Shangsi Festival

## Overview

The Shangsi Festival falls on the third day of the third lunar month. It was once called Chong San, San Yue San, or the Day of Purification. In earlier times the date was the first si day of the third lunar month; from the Wei and Jin period it settled on the third day. People went to the water to wash, to drink and to walk in the spring countryside. It is now seldom observed among Han Chinese, while in Guangxi and neighbouring areas the same date is kept as a large song festival.

## Origins

Shangsi grew out of the pre-Qin rite of fuxi, a purification bath taken by running water. The Rites of Zhou records that the female shaman was in charge of seasonal purification and of bathing with fragrant herbs, and the Analects has Zeng Xi describe his wish to bathe in the Yi River in late spring, stand in the wind on the rain altar and go home singing. By the Han dynasty the day was a fixed observance: the Book of the Later Han states that on the first si day of the third month officials and commoners alike washed in the eastward-flowing water to remove old grime and illness. From the Wei and Jin period the date was simplified to the third day of the third month, and the Record of the Seasons of Jing and Chu notes that people went out to pools and sandbars for the floating-cup drinking of the winding stream, so that the old purification rite turned into a spring outing.

## Customs

1. **Purification by water**: washing at a river or lake to remove dirt and, in old belief, illness and ill fortune.
2. **Floating cups along a winding stream**: guests sit beside a curved channel while a cup is set adrift upstream; whoever it stops before drinks and composes a poem.
3. **Spring outings**: in mild weather men and women left the city for the waterside, gathering orchids and wearing fragrant herbs.
4. **Eggs boiled with shepherd's purse**: in Hunan and Hubei, old flowering shepherd's purse is boiled with eggs.
5. **Song fairs**: in Guangxi the third day of the third month brings antiphonal singing, embroidered ball tossing and five-colour glutinous rice.

## Story

According to the Preface to the Orchid Pavilion Collection, on the third day of the third month in 353, the ninth year of the Yonghe era, Wang Xizhi gathered with Xie An, Sun Chuo and more than forty others at the Orchid Pavilion in Shanyin, Kuaiji, for the purification rite. Seated along a winding stream, they passed cups round and composed verses; Wang wrote the preface on the spot, and its line about using the water as a winding course for floating cups became the classic reference for the custom. A separate tradition holds that the date is the birthday of the Yellow Emperor, and ceremonies are still held in Xinzheng, Henan, on this day.

## Theme

Only a small cup floating on water, with three ripples spreading outward from it. Cup and ripples take up very little room and the rest is empty, like the instant at the Orchid Pavilion when the stream carried a cup to someone's knees. The rings widening outward also carry the sense of gathering and parting.

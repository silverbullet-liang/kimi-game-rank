# July 7 Incident

## Overview

The July 7 Incident is the Sino-Japanese military clash that broke out near the Marco Polo Bridge, southwest of Beiping, on 7 July 1937. In Chinese it is called qiqi shibian, the July 7 Incident, or Lugouqiao shibian, the Marco Polo Bridge Incident; in Japanese, 七・七事変（盧溝橋事件）. It is generally treated as the starting point of the whole-nation War of Resistance. Commemorations are held in a number of Chinese cities each 7 July.

## Origins

On the night of 7 July 1937 Japanese troops were conducting exercises north of the Marco Polo Bridge. After the exercise the Japanese side reported a soldier missing and asked to search the walled town of Wanping; the Chinese garrison refused. Firing followed around the bridge and the town. On 8 July the Communist Party of China issued a nationwide telegram calling for resistance, and the Nationalist military authorities moved troops north. On 17 July Chiang Kai-shek spoke at Lushan and set out his "last resort" position. In late July Japanese forces attacked towards Beiping and Tianjin, which fell on 29 and 30 July. On 13 August fighting began at Shanghai, and the war spread from north China to the east.

## Customs

1. **Commemorative ceremonies**: the Museum of the War of Chinese People's Resistance Against Japanese Aggression and sites at the Marco Polo Bridge hold observances and lay flowers on 7 July.
2. **Air-raid sirens**: some cities test their civil defence sirens on this date.
3. **Visits and exhibitions**: special exhibitions at the bridge, at Wanping and at the museum open through the summer.
4. **Archives and seminars**: archive offices and universities release newly arranged documents and oral records and hold academic meetings.
5. **School activities**: visits, film screenings, reading and class sessions.

## Story

Accounts of the night itself differ between the two sides. The received narrative runs roughly as follows: after the Japanese exercise near the bridge, the Japanese force asked to enter Wanping on the grounds of a missing soldier; the Chinese 29th Army unit holding the town declined, and negotiation went on alongside the firing. On 17 July Chiang Kai-shek made his "last resort" statement at Lushan. Beiping fell on 29 July, and by August the fighting had reached Shanghai. Shell marks on the wall of Wanping were kept after the war. On the details of that night the Chinese and Japanese records differ, and historians continue to compare documents.

## Theme

This skin draws a stretch of crenellated wall along the foot of the page, with a breach broken through its middle section and a line of dark red inside the breach. The battlements are even and evenly spaced until they stop at the gap; the dark red stays within the opening and does not spill outward. There is no fire and no figure in the picture, and more is left empty than is drawn, so what remains is a skyline whose height no longer agrees with itself.

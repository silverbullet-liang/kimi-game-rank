# Xinhai Revolution

## Overview

10 October marks the Xinhai Revolution. On the night of 10 October 1911, the nineteenth day of the eighth month of the Xinhai year, the Wuchang Uprising broke out. The revolution that followed ended Qing rule, and on 1 January 1912 the Provisional Government of the Republic of China was established in Nanjing with Sun Yat-sen as provisional president. The tenth of October has been observed ever since, and in Taiwan it is kept as Double Ten Day.

## Origins

In the summer of 1911 the Railway Protection movement in Sichuan led the Qing court to move part of the Hubei New Army into the province, thinning the garrison at Wuhan. Two revolutionary groups in Hubei, the Literary Society and the Progressive Assembly, united with encouragement from the Tongmenghui's central office; on 24 September they resolved to rise on 6 October, a date later postponed. On 9 October Sun Wu was making bombs at a headquarters in the Russian concession in Hankou when one exploded; documents were seized, the premises were exposed, arrests followed a name list, and Peng Chufan, Liu Fuji and Yang Hongsheng were killed. On the evening of 10 October the revolutionaries of the New Army's 8th Engineer Battalion moved first, took the armory at Chuwangtai by the Zhonghe Gate, and made Wu Zhaolin acting commander; after a night of fighting they took the governor-general's office. The Hubei military government was formed on the 11th and the three towns of Wuhan were in their hands by the 12th. The Zhonghe Gate was renamed Uprising Gate in 1912.

## Customs

1. **Visiting Uprising Gate**: the old Zhonghe Gate in Wuchang, with its gate tower and stretch of wall, is treated as the starting point of the rising.
2. **The museums**: the former Hubei military government building, known as the Red Building, is now a museum and was listed among the first national key cultural sites in 1961; the Xinhai Revolution Museum opened beside Shouyi Square in 2011, the centenary year.
3. **Ceremonies and conferences**: observances are held on the round decades, with scholarly conferences and documentary publication alongside.
4. **Flowers and exhibitions**: at the martyrs' shrine on Peng-Liu-Yang Road and at the gate tower, flowers and photographic displays are set out around the date.
5. **Two observances**: the mainland calls it the anniversary of the Xinhai Revolution, Taiwan keeps 10 October as Double Ten Day, each with its own ceremonies.

## Story

Accounts differ on who fired the first shot that night, with Xiong Bingkun and Cheng Zhengying among the names given, and historians hold different grounds. What is certain is that the 8th Engineer Battalion started it, that the objective was the armory at Chuwangtai and that the weapons stored there equipped the fighting that followed, that the governor-general's office was attacked three times in the night and taken before dawn, and that a provincial capital changed hands in a single night. Most of those who led it were junior officers and soldiers; the men who had been meant to lead were arrested, gone or wounded, and the military government set up afterwards made Li Yuanhong its governor, a turn that was argued over at the time.

## Theme

The skin uses dark red and iron grey in horizontal blocks, pressed one over another, with hard straight edges and no curves. The red is not festive; it is closer to rust and iron. The gaps left between the blocks make the whole picture look as though it has been shoved a step forward. The rising happened at night, and the picture stays in the night.

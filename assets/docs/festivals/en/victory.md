# Victory Day of the Chinese People's War of Resistance Against Japanese Aggression

## Overview

3 September is the Victory Day of the Chinese People's War of Resistance Against Japanese Aggression. On 2 September 1945 Japan signed the instrument of surrender aboard the battleship Missouri in Tokyo Bay, and 3 September was fixed as the victory day. On 27 February 2014 the Standing Committee of the 12th National People's Congress determined 3 September as the victory day by legislative decision and provided that state commemorations be held each year.

## Origins

On 15 August 1945 Japan announced its acceptance of the Potsdam Declaration, and on 2 September it signed the instrument of surrender. The Chinese government of the time designated 3 September as the victory day and commemorations were held from 1946. On 13 August 1951 the Government Administration Council of the Central People's Government issued a circular confirming 3 September as the anniversary of victory in the war of resistance. On 27 February 2014 the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress adopted a decision confirming 3 September as the Victory Day by legislation, with state commemorations each year.

## Customs

1. **State ceremonies**: on 3 September ceremonies are held at the Museum of the War of Chinese People's Resistance Against Japanese Aggression and elsewhere, with flowers laid for the war dead.
2. **Silence and sirens**: a moment of silence at the ceremony, and air-raid sirens sounded in some cities.
3. **Exhibitions and documents**: exhibitions on the war, releases of archival material and oral history projects come out mainly in the autumn.
4. **Schools**: visits to memorial halls, film screenings, reading of wartime records and class sessions.
5. **Private remembrance**: groups lay flowers and plant trees at cemeteries and wartime sites.

## Story

On the morning of 2 September 1945, on the deck of the battleship Missouri in Tokyo Bay, Foreign Minister Shigemitsu Mamoru signed the instrument of surrender for the emperor and the Japanese government and General Umezu Yoshijiro for the imperial general headquarters. The Allied representatives MacArthur and Nimitz, and the Chinese delegate Xu Yongchang, signed in turn. When the news reached China, celebrations were held in Chongqing, Yan'an and elsewhere. 3 September has been the victory day on the Chinese side since then. The 1951 circular kept the date, and the legislative decision of 2014 kept it as well.

## Theme

This skin holds a single horizon line, with a half arc of warm light slowly coming up behind it. The line is straight and runs to both edges; the arc sits at its centre and the light shows through from below the line, brightest where it touches it and fainter at each remove above. There are no fireworks, no cheering and no flags. There is one way for the page to be lit, and that is from behind the line.

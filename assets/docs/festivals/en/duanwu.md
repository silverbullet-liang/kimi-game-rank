# Dragon Boat Festival

## Overview

The fifth day of the fifth lunar month, also called the Double Fifth, the Duan Yang Festival, the Dragon Boat Festival or the Orchid Bath Festival. With the Spring Festival, Qingming and the Mid-Autumn Festival it counts among the four major traditional festivals of China. In May 2006 the State Council placed it on the first national list of intangible cultural heritage; from 2008 it has been a statutory holiday; and in September 2009 UNESCO inscribed the Chinese festival on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

## Origins

The term duanwu appears in the Records of Local Customs by Zhou Chu of the Western Jin. According to the Record of the Seasons of Jing and Chu by Zong Lin of the Liang, the fifth month is midsummer and its first wu day is called duan yang, which is why the fifth day of the fifth month is also known as the Duan Yang Festival. Three explanations of its origin are commonly given: the dragon totem rites and boat racing of the ancient Yue peoples of the lower Yangzi; the treatment of the fifth month as an evil month, met with herb gathering, orchid baths and mugwort hung at the door; and the commemoration of Qu Yuan, who drowned himself in the Miluo River, the version most widely told. Over time the three merged into the customs kept today.

## Customs

1. **Boat racing**: crews row to a drum, and races are held on rivers and lakes.
2. **Wrapping zongzi**: glutinous rice wrapped in reed leaves, plain or with dates, bean paste or salted pork, tied into four-cornered or pyramidal shapes and boiled.
3. **Mugwort and calamus**: hung beside the door, with fragrant sachets worn and realgar wine drunk.
4. **Five-colour threads**: tied around children's wrists, in old belief against harm.
5. **Orchid baths and herb gathering**: water boiled with orchid and mugwort for bathing, and herbs picked around midday.

## Story

According to the Historical Records, Qu Yuan was a minister of Chu who urged alliance with Qi against Qin and was banished after slander. In 278 BC, when Qin took the Chu capital Ying, he drowned himself in the Miluo. The Supplementary Record of Qi and Xie by Wu Jun of the Liang tells that the people of Chu, in grief, threw bamboo tubes filled with rice into the river each year; later Qu Yuan appeared to a man and said the offering was often seized by a river dragon, and that chinaberry leaves stoppering the tube and five-colour threads would frighten it. Hence rice wrapped in leaves and bound with thread. Boat racing is likewise said to come from the boats that set out to rescue him.

## Theme

A pyramidal zongzi with sharp corners, bound with string, and three lines of water beneath it. The folds of the leaf are straight and the water lines curve, hard against soft, which is the festival in two halves: the handwork of wrapping and tying, and the water that carries the boats.

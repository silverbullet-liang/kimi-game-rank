# 1024 Programmer's Day

## Overview

24 October is the working date of China's Programmer's Day. 1024 is two to the tenth power and the base of storage arithmetic: one kilobyte is 1024 bytes, one megabyte is 1024 kilobytes. Written as 10/24 the date has the same shape as the number. It is not a statutory holiday; it was settled by technical communities and the people who work in them, and each company and community arranges it for itself.

## Origins

The established Programmer's Day comes from Russia. In 2002 the programmer Valentin Balt collected signatures on a petition to the government; on 11 September 2009 President Dmitry Medvedev signed the measure making the 256th day of the year the holiday, which falls on 13 September in ordinary years and 12 September in leap years. 256 is the count a single byte can express, so the date moves. China took a different route. Around 2010 the communities around Cnblogs, CSDN and similar sites ran discussions and polls on fixing 24 October as the Chinese date; around 2015 training institutions and internet companies took it up and it became a fixture in the industry. Since 2020 Changsha has held an annual 1024 programmer festival with forums and exhibition floors.

## Customs

1. **Company events**: technical talks, internal contests and gifts, and in some firms an annual open day for engineering.
2. **Forums and developer conferences**: releases cluster around the date, and commits to open repositories rise.
3. **The no-overtime pledge**: some organisations use the day to send staff home on time, a proposal better remembered than the holiday itself.
4. **Marking it with work**: writing a line, making a commit, closing an old bug.
5. **Number jokes**: 1024, and the pun that reads a gigabyte as a word for "first rate".

## Story

The person usually called the first programmer is Ada Lovelace. In 1843, in the notes she wrote for a paper on Babbage's Analytical Engine, she set out a sequence of steps for computing Bernoulli numbers, now treated as one of the earliest descriptions of an algorithm; in the same notes she asked whether the engine might compose music, more than a century before a machine did. The link to this day is that a programmer's holiday, traced far enough back, arrives at the notes of a woman writing about a machine that was never built.

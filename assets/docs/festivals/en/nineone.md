# September 18 Incident

## Overview

At about ten at night on 18 September 1931, the Japanese Kwantung Army blew up a section of the South Manchuria Railway near Liutiaohu, north of Shenyang, blamed it on Chinese troops, and on that pretext shelled the Northeast Army barracks at Beidaying and attacked the city. Shenyang fell before dawn the next day, and by February 1932 the whole of Manchuria was occupied. China dates its War of Resistance from 1931 to 1945, fourteen years in all. The 18th of September is observed as the anniversary of the incident.

## Origins

Before the incident the South Manchuria Railway was operated by Japan, with Kwantung Army units stationed along the line. On the night of 18 September 1931 the army detonated explosives on the track at Liutiaohu and moved at once, citing Chinese sabotage, against Beidaying and Shenyang. Zhang Xueliang, then acting head of the Northeast military administration, and the Northeast authorities followed a policy of non-resistance, and most of the Northeast Army withdrew without fighting; the city changed hands in a single night. Over the following months Japanese forces advanced along the railway, taking Heilongjiang in February 1932 and completing the occupation of the region. The Chinese side counts the armed resistance from 1931 onward, fourteen years in total, a reckoning that has been standard in textbooks and official commemorations since 2017.

## Customs

1. **Air-raid sirens**: since 1995 Shenyang has sounded air-raid sirens across the city at 9:18 on 18 September for three minutes, with vehicles stopping and sounding horns; from 2006 all fourteen cities in Liaoning joined, and more than a hundred cities elsewhere now do the same.
2. **The bell**: a bell-ringing ceremony is held in the square of the September 18 History Museum in Shenyang, where the warning bell is struck fourteen times, one for each year.
3. **The museum**: it was founded on 18 September 1991, the sixtieth anniversary, and the present building opened on 18 September 1999; its east wall stands next to the old Beidaying barracks, about eight hundred metres from the Liutiaohu section of track.
4. **The old sites**: the Beidaying site is open as an exhibition hall, and a second museum at the Baita Park in Liaoyang marks the place where the incident was planned.
5. **Schools and public observances**: moments of silence, wreaths, lectures and exhibitions are held in the days around the date.

## Story

In the square of the September 18 History Museum in Shenyang stands a monument shaped like an open desk calendar. The date on the right-hand page is fixed at 18 September 1931, a Friday; the left-hand page carries an account of the incident; the surface is pitted with holes like bullet marks. He Zhongling, a professor at the Lu Xun Academy of Fine Arts who took part in the design, has explained that the base uses skull and bone forms and the body is made as an open calendar, so that the page cannot be turned. The monument went up with the museum in 1991, and the annual ceremony is held beside it.

## Theme

The skin sets a block of calendar squares at the upper right and cuts a single crack from top to bottom through the middle one, so that the date inside it breaks apart. What splits is not the paper but the continuity a calendar is meant to hold, and a skin made for one day draws that day as a break.

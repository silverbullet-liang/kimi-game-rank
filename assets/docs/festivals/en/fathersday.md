# Father's Day

## Overview

The third Sunday of June is the most widely used date for Father's Day, and it is the day marked in the United States, mainland China and Japan, among others. It is not a statutory holiday, and not every place keeps it on that date: Taiwan uses 8 August, because the words for "eight eight" and "baba", daddy, sound alike; Thailand keeps it on 5 December, and Spain and several other countries on 19 March.

## Origins

The modern holiday began in the United States. In 1909 Sonora Smart Dodd of Spokane, Washington, came away from a Mother's Day service and asked her minister why there was no day for fathers. Her mother had died giving birth to the sixth child, and her father, William Smart, a Civil War veteran, raised the six children alone on a farm in the countryside. On 19 June 1910 Spokane held the first Father's Day observance. Over the following decades the idea was taken up more widely: President Coolidge gave it his support in 1924, President Johnson proclaimed June a month of fatherhood in 1966, and in 1972 President Nixon signed a measure making the third Sunday of June a nationwide Father's Day.

## Customs

1. **Flowers**: a red rose or a dendrobium orchid for a father still living, white flowers in memory of one who has died, following Mrs Dodd's own usage.
2. **Gifts and cards**: children write cards and prepare small presents, and schools organise craft and essay activities.
3. **Family meals**: sons and daughters cook or order in, and the father stays out of the kitchen for the day.
4. **Calls from a distance**: telephoning or video calling a father who lives in another city or country.
5. **Community events**: in some cities June brings father-and-child runs, family sports days and charity sales.

## Story

In the spring of 1910 Mrs Dodd worked with her minister, the Reverend Rasmus, and the local Young Women's Christian Association to prepare the first Father's Day. The date was set for her father's birthday, 5 June, but preparations ran late and the observance was moved to the third Sunday of the month, 19 June. The local newspaper recorded the service. For the next sixty-two years Mrs Dodd kept writing letters and giving talks, pressing businesses, churches and successive presidents, until Father's Day became a nationwide observance in the United States in 1972. She died in 1978, having seen it happen.

# Journalists' Day

## Overview

8 November is China's Journalists' Day. On 1 August 2000 the State Council approved the request of the All-China Journalists Association on fixing the date, and 8 November was set; the first observance was on 8 November that year. With Nurses' Day and Teachers' Day it is one of the three professional days in the country, and under State Council rules it is a working day rather than a holiday.

## Origins

The date is that of the association's predecessor. On the evening of 8 November 1937, at the initiative of Fan Changjiang and twenty-three others, the Chinese Young Journalists' Association was founded at the Nanjing Hotel, 200 Shannxi South Road, Shanghai. After the city fell it moved to Wuhan, held its first national congress at Hankou on 30 March 1938 and was later renamed; the All-China Journalists Association was formed in 1957, and in 1987 it concluded that its origins go back to 8 November 1937. The 1949 measures on national holidays already listed a journalists' day but set no date; the measures revised on 18 September 1999 listed it again; the association submitted its request on 25 January 2000, and approval came on 1 August. Between 1933 and 1949 the press in China had observed 1 September instead, following a circular from the Hangzhou journalists' guild in 1934.

## Customs

1. **Awards**: the China News Awards and the Changjiang-Taofen Awards are announced or presented around the date.
2. **Meetings**: the association and news organisations hold discussions and professional seminars.
3. **Reporting from the ground**: many outlets schedule the day as a day in the field rather than in a meeting room.
4. **Open days and exhibitions**: newsrooms open to visitors, and press photography shows are mounted at the same time.
5. **Self-examination**: trade publications use the date for pieces on reporting ethics, fact-checking and the limits of the work.

## Story

Full-scale war began in July 1937. In the middle of that month Zhou Enlai, then in Shanghai on behalf of the Party centre, put the idea of a united front of patriotic journalists to Hu Yuzhi, Xia Yan and others. On 4 November Fan Changjiang met Hu Yuzhi, Xia Yan and Yun Yiqun and the decision to form the body was taken; at seven in the evening on 8 November the founding meeting opened at the Nanjing Hotel, officers were elected, and a journal, *The Journalist*, was planned. The association went on to establish more than forty branches and at its height counted over two thousand members. In 2010 the hotel where it began was listed as a protected site.

## Theme

The skin is newsprint white against lead black. Three very faint column rules run down each side and divide the page into columns; a hand-drawn vermilion circle sits at the upper right, going round without closing, leaving a gap; a steel pen nib at the lower right points upward. The red appears only there, and it is the colour of a margin note, not of celebration.

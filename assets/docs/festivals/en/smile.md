# World Smile Day

## Overview

Marked on 8 May. In Chinese usage World Smile Day shares its date with World Red Cross and Red Crescent Day, which commemorates the birth of Henry Dunant, founder of the International Red Cross movement. Smiley motifs, street charity and talks on mental health are used on the day to draw attention to mood and to relations between people.

## Origins

8 May is Henry Dunant's birthday, in 1828. In 1948 the Council of the League of Red Cross Societies designated the date World Red Cross Day, later World Red Cross and Red Crescent Day. Chinese sources commonly add that in the same year the World Federation for Mental Health established 8 May as World Smile Day, so that smiling might promote well-being and pass friendliness from person to person.

There is a second day under the same English name. In 1963 the commercial artist Harvey Ball drew the yellow smiley face; uneasy at how heavily the image was sold, he proposed a World Smile Day, first held in his home town in 1999 and since fixed on the first Friday of October, under the idea of doing one act of kindness and helping one person smile. The two dates differ, and the Chinese World Smile Day normally means 8 May.

## Customs

1. **Smiley motifs**: drawing faces, handing out stickers, painting balloons and paper fans.
2. **Street charity**: volunteers giving smile cards to passers-by and taking photographs.
3. **Mental health talks**: mood regulation, stress management, sleep and social contact.
4. **School activities**: class meetings, smile walls, notes of encouragement to classmates.
5. **Visits**: calling at care homes, hospitals and children's welfare centres.

## Story

In 1963 Harvey Ball, a commercial artist in Worcester, Massachusetts, was commissioned by an insurance company and in about ten minutes drew a yellow circle with two dots and a curve. He was paid forty-five dollars. By many accounts the face then went onto badges, T-shirts and stamps and became the expression of an era. Ball later grew uneasy about how far the image was used to sell things, argued that a day should be set aside for goodwill, and in 1999 his home town held the first World Smile Day.

## Theme

Only a curve and two dots: the curve bending upward, the dots set symmetrically above it, and nothing else at all. No outline, no shading, no caption. A face reduced as far as it can go, and the little information left is enough, which is exactly how a smile is recognised.

# National Day

## Overview

1 October is the National Day of the People's Republic of China. At three in the afternoon on 1 October 1949 the founding ceremony was held in Tiananmen Square and the Central People's Government was proclaimed. On 2 December 1949 the fourth meeting of the Central People's Government Council adopted the Resolution on the National Day of the People's Republic of China, following a proposal by the first National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference, and fixed 1 October as the date each year. This skin belongs to a nine-day window, shown from 30 September to 8 October, which carries Martyrs' Day and the National Day holiday in one stretch.

## Origins

At three in the afternoon on 1 October 1949 the ceremony began. Mao Zedong read the proclamation from the rostrum of Tiananmen, then pressed a switch to raise the first five-star red flag, fifty-four saluting guns fired twenty-eight rounds, and a military parade and mass procession followed. A parade was held every year from 1949 to 1959; after that the practice followed a rhythm of small observances every five years and large ones every ten, with parades in 1984, 1999, 2009 and 2019. In 1999 the State Council revised the Measures for National Holidays and Memorial Days, raising the statutory leave for National Day from one day to three, which with shifted rest days makes seven; the long holiday dates from then. In some years the holiday has overlapped with the Mid-Autumn Festival.

## Customs

1. **Raising the flag**: the ceremony in Tiananmen Square is timed to sunrise, and the crowd is largest on 1 October.
2. **Parade and procession**: held in the years of major observance, with foot formations, vehicle columns and aircraft passing the square in turn.
3. **Flags on buildings**: offices, schools, shops and households hang the flag at doors and windows, and streets line up in rows of red.
4. **Travel**: the seven-day break is one of the two busiest travel periods of the year for railways, roads and scenic sites.
5. **Flower beds and lights**: a themed flower display is installed in Tiananmen Square each year and lit at night.

## Story

Three details of the founding ceremony are most often recalled. The three o'clock start was chosen with air defence in mind. The flag was raised by an electric mechanism: Mao pressed the switch and the flag climbed the pole. And the fifty-four guns fired twenty-eight rounds as the flag went up. Some three hundred thousand people filled the square that day, and four of the aircraft in the flypast carried live bombs. These details come from the reporting of the time and from later oral accounts, and they remain the usual way into that afternoon.

## Theme

The skin works in two colours, red as ground and gold as line and edge, with geometric blocks arranged across them like an abstraction of flags, lanterns and streamers. It points at no single object; it only raises the density of celebration. Because the window runs nine days, this colour has to sit among ordinary days on both sides, joining the quiet theme before it and the one after.

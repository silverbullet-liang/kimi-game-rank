# Qixi Festival

## Overview

The seventh day of the seventh lunar month is the Qixi Festival, also called the Festival of Skills, the Daughters' Festival or the Double Seventh. It is tied to the story of the Herd Boy and the Weaver Girl and is the traditional festival most closely bound up with women, handwork and the stars. In 2006 it was entered on the first national list of intangible cultural heritage. Japan, the Korean peninsula and Vietnam keep observances of the same origin, on different dates and in different ways.

## Origins

Qixi begins in the observation of the stars. The Herd Boy and the Weaver Girl lie on opposite banks of the Milky Way, and the Book of Songs already has lines on the Weaver and the Herd Boy. The Han period joined the seventh day of the seventh month to needle-threading as a test of skill: the Miscellaneous Records of the Western Capital describes palace women threading a seven-holed needle at the Kaijin Tower on that day. After the Eastern Han the two stars were increasingly given human form as husband and wife, and the Nineteen Old Poems already carry the longing of the distant Herd Boy and the bright River Han woman. From the Jin dynasty on, Qixi was a fixed seasonal observance, and the customs of praying for skill and for children flourished in the Tang and Song.

## Customs

1. **Threading a needle**: by moonlight women pass coloured thread through a seven-holed needle, and the quickest is held to have been granted skill.
2. **Tables of offerings**: in the courtyard a table is set with moulded cakes, melon and fruit, lotus root and flowers, sometimes called the table for praying for skill.
3. **Watching the stars**: after dark people find the two stars on either side of the Milky Way, and older relatives tell the story to children.
4. **Washing hair and dyeing nails**: in some places hair is washed in water boiled with cypress leaves and peach twigs, and nails are dyed with balsam.
5. **How it is kept now**: flowers, dates and marriage registration, with group weddings and craft markets in some cities.

## Story

As the tale is told, the Herd Boy was an orphaned cowherd and the Weaver Girl a weaver of brocade in heaven. They married and had a boy and a girl, he farming and she weaving. When the Queen Mother of the West learned of it she took the Weaver Girl back to heaven, and the Herd Boy followed wearing the hide of his old ox; the Queen Mother drew her gold hairpin behind him and cut a river of stars across the sky, leaving the two on opposite banks. Magpies build a bridge on the seventh night of the seventh month so that they may meet once a year. The story reached its full form in texts and storytelling after the Han, and details vary from place to place; the meeting on the magpie bridge is the best known part.

## Theme

This skin puts a slanting river of stars across the head of the page, with one bright rayed star on each bank. The river runs from upper left to lower right, and the two stars sit at different heights and at unequal distances from it. The rays are very fine and four-pointed, neither twinkling nor moving. The whole plot of Qixi lies in that distance: a band of light that cannot be crossed, with a star at either end.

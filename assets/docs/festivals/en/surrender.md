# Surrender of Japan

## Overview

On 15 August 1945 Japan announced its acceptance of the Potsdam Declaration, the event generally called the surrender of Japan and known in Japanese as 日本の降伏; in Japan the day is also called the day the war ended. On 2 September of the same year Japan signed the instrument of surrender aboard the United States battleship Missouri in Tokyo Bay. 15 August is observed as a commemorative day in China and Korea, while in Japan a national memorial ceremony for the war dead is held on the date.

## Origins

On 26 July 1945 the United States, Britain and China issued the Potsdam Declaration, calling for Japan's unconditional surrender. Atomic bombs were dropped on Hiroshima on 6 August and on Nagasaki on 9 August; on 9 August the Soviet Union declared war on Japan and sent troops into northeast China. On 14 August the Japanese government notified the Allied powers that it accepted the declaration. At noon on 15 August the recorded Imperial Rescript on the termination of the war was broadcast, announcing that acceptance. On the morning of 2 September Foreign Minister Shigemitsu Mamoru and General Umezu Yoshijiro signed the instrument aboard the Missouri, followed by the Allied representatives including the Chinese delegate Xu Yongchang, and the Second World War came to an end.

## Customs

1. **Commemorations**: observances are held in many Chinese cities on 15 August; since 2014, 3 September has been the Victory Day of the Chinese People's War of Resistance Against Japanese Aggression, marked by state ceremonies.
2. **Memorial for the war dead**: held on 15 August at the Nippon Budokan in Tokyo, attended by the emperor, the prime minister and bereaved families.
3. **Bells and silence**: some cities ring bells at noon or observe a minute of silence.
4. **Documents and testimony**: archives and museums release papers, photographs and the broadcast recording connected with the surrender.
5. **Private remembrance**: groups lay flowers at war memorial sites and cemeteries.

## Story

Late on 14 August and into the small hours of 15 August, in a recording room in Tokyo, the Showa Emperor recorded the rescript on the end of the war. That night some officers tried to seize the records and stop the broadcast, and did not succeed. At noon on 15 August the recording was played, and the announcer first explained that the voice was the emperor's own. In the same period Japanese units across China received orders to cease fire, though fighting continued in some areas after 15 August, and surrender ceremonies went on into September and later. On 9 September the surrender ceremony for the China theatre was held at Nanjing, and General Okamura Yasuji, commander of the Japanese China Expeditionary Army, signed the document.
